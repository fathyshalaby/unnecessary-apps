# Unnecessary Apps — creator and release evaluation

Date: 30 August 2026  
Scope: all 44 active app concepts and their current SwiftUI implementations. The former Lab Report Translator is retired and excluded from release scope.

For the harsher product-versus-episode decision, read the companion [critical portfolio audit](CRITICAL_PORTFOLIO_AUDIT.md). This document remains the directional creator scorecard; it should not be read as a promise that every high-scoring episode deserves a standalone App Store release.

## Verdict

The collection is strongest as a serialized creator project, not as 44 simultaneous App Store launches. The best episodes have three things in one glance: a painfully recognizable problem, a physical or social test the viewer can watch, and a decisive result they can argue with.

The first social season should lead with **Chair Finder**, then move through **Do Not Text Them**, **The Door Was Push**, **Receipt Emotional Damage**, **Meeting Bingo for One**, and **Apology Draft Generator**. These have the clearest hooks and the cleanest screen-recording payoff.

The health-adjacent apps should be a clearly labeled later arc. They are useful as creator content, but they carry more trust, wording, and App Review risk than the silly social apps.

## Scoring method

The score is a directional creator score out of 5, not a prediction of views. It weighs:

- **Hook:** can a stranger understand the premise in one second?
- **Demo:** is there a satisfying action/result to film?
- **Comment loop:** does it invite stories, votes, arguments, or user submissions?
- **Ship safety:** is the current implementation honest about what it does and free of unnecessary permissions?

## Product and App Store strategy

Use all 44 active apps as episodes, but do not promise all 44 as immediate public App Store releases. The collection contains several adjacent utilities and several deliberately tiny experiences. Apple’s guidelines warn against minimum-functionality and spam-like submissions, so validate the audience with one pilot and release the strongest standalone apps in waves. See the [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

The health arc (36–41, 43, and 44) should have its own disclaimer and review pass. The outdoor/civic arc (01, 02, 23, 29) should never imply live data or permissions that the current builds do not use.
