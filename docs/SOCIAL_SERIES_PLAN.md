# Unnecessary Apps — social series plan

## Brand decision

**Public series:** **Unnecessary Apps**  
**Studio:** **Unnecessary Apps Corp**  
**Creator line:** “I build apps for problems that did not need solving.”  
**Recurring stamp:** `UNNECESSARY APP · S01 E01`

The name is not **Unnecessary Inventions** and it is not **Apps Nobody Asked For**. The recognizable genre is “over-engineering tiny problems,” but the ownable expression is software: polished mobile apps, mascots, fake departments, official verdicts, and the creator building everything in public.

Use the correct spelling **Unnecessary** everywhere public. Keep the existing one-n spelling only in the external-drive path and current bundle identifiers until a deliberate migration.

Do not display internal Xcode target numbers such as App35. Social episode numbers describe publication order, allowing the strongest apps to launch first.

## The promise

Every episode answers one question:

> What if a painfully specific human problem had a suspiciously polished app?

The main episode is a complete builder story rather than a direct-response ad:
the viewer sees the problem, hears why the creator cared, watches the solution
take shape, sees the real app work, and gets an honest verdict. The viewer
should understand the premise in the opening seconds, but the payoff can take
75–105 seconds because the story—not the download—is the product.

The detailed format, pilot script, and Seedance/Higgsfield production rules are
in [`docs/SOCIAL_STORY_FORMAT.md`](SOCIAL_STORY_FORMAT.md). The existing short
scripts are now cutdown material for Stories, Shorts, and alternate hooks.

## Repeatable episode format

```text
[0–6s]    Cold open: physical problem or final result first.
[6–18s]   Personal story: what happened and why it bothered the creator.
[18–32s]  Solution premise: what the app should do and why it is overbuilt.
[32–48s]  Build/process: sketches, decisions, rejected ideas, or testing.
[48–78s]  Real app walkthrough: input, action, result.
[78–94s]  Honest verdict: useful, silly, limited, or not ready.
[94–105s] Soft CTA: “If you want to try it, check the link in my bio.”
```

Each opening needs three simultaneous hooks:

- **Visual:** physical prop, awkward situation, or absurd result.
- **Verbal:** one sentence naming the tiny pain.
- **Text:** `I BUILT AN APP FOR ___` or the result itself.

Do not begin with a logo animation, “hey guys,” a coding montage, or an explanation of the series. The joke comes first; the brand stamp appears immediately after it.

## Content pillars

1. **Tiny problem, corporate response** — the main app reveal.
2. **I actually built it** — UI, mascot, bugs, rejected names, and implementation decisions.
3. **Field testing nonsense** — the app confronts a real chair, sock, door, snack, or consenting friend.
4. **Internet Court** — comments submit evidence and decide whether the app deserves release.
5. **Release Desk** — honest TestFlight status, App Review outcomes, and changes made from feedback.

The main feed should be roughly 60% reveals, 20% field tests, 15% build/process, and 5% direct promotion. Stories can carry daily polls, icon votes, outtakes, and progress.

## Platforms and cadence

Create one clean 9:16 master with original audio and burned-in captions, then publish it to TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts without a platform watermark.

- **Monday, Wednesday, Friday:** one Unnecessary App reveal.
- **Tuesday or Thursday:** one low-effort Story/poll using footage from the reveal.
- **Sunday:** 20-minute analytics review and next-week hook decision.
- **LinkedIn:** only office-flavored episodes or thoughtful build-in-public posts.

Initial test slot: **18:30 Vienna time**. Keep it consistent for the first nine episodes, then use actual audience data to test a second slot. Do not optimize a posting time before there is an audience.

## Release strategy

Social validation and App Store release are separate lanes. The video can exist before the app is downloadable.

- **No public build:** “Follow to see whether this survives App Review.”
- **Internal TestFlight:** “I’m testing it with a small group; I’ll share the next build when it stops misbehaving.”
- **Public TestFlight:** “The test link is in the bio; expect bugs with excellent branding.”
- **App Store:** “It is unfortunately real. Link in bio.”

Ship Chair Finder first. After that, prioritize apps that produce both strong retention and explicit download demand. Do not upload all 44 as near-simultaneous tiny apps; several should remain social episodes, become features inside a collection app, or earn release through audience response.

## Production workflow

Batch the following every Saturday in one three-to-four-hour session:

1. Lock the personal problem, solution premise, limitation, and soft close.
2. Record the voiceover and real app walkthrough before generating any AI footage.
3. Film physical cold opens and reactions in one lighting setup.
4. Use Higgsfield/Seedance 2.5 for the Soul presenter and supporting cutaways;
   do not use generated footage for exact app UI or readable feature claims.
5. Assemble one 75–105 second story master, then cut a 60-second version and a
   20-second result hook from the same source.
6. Export a clean master, thumbnail frame, and a six-second Story cutdown.
7. Schedule the main story; use Stories and comment replies to carry the CTA.

Required shot pack per app:

- one physical problem shot;
- one close-up of the prop;
- one clean app interaction;
- one official result screen;
- one deadpan reaction;
- one alternate hook for testing; and
- one still image for thumbnail/cover.

## Comment and community loop

Pin one question, not a generic “thoughts?” The best prompts ask for evidence:

- “What should the app judge next?”
- “What is your exact version of this problem?”
- “What result would make this scientifically correct?”
- “Should I release this or delete the source code?”

Reply to the strongest comments with video. Credit the commenter on screen when their idea becomes an episode. Never manufacture a user reaction or imply an app is live when it is not.

## Weekly scorecard

Review each episode after seven days:

- first-second and three-second hold;
- average percentage watched and completion rate;
- shares and sends per 1,000 views;
- comments that contain a personal story or app suggestion;
- follows and profile visits per 1,000 views;
- “where can I download it?” comments;
- TestFlight link taps, installs, and second-session opens when available.

The winning episode is not merely the one with the most views. It should make the premise obvious, earn a share or argument, and create either download demand or the next app idea.
