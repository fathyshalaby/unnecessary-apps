# Unnecessary Apps — creator-story video format

## Creative direction

The main video is a builder story, not an advertisement. It follows the creator
from a small real annoyance to a deliberately overbuilt app, then lets the
viewer decide whether the app deserved to exist.

The reference is the high-level “unnecessary invention” genre: a personable
creator notices a tiny problem, proposes a suspiciously serious solution, shows
the making of it, and tests the result. The expression here must stay original:
software instead of physical inventions, a Vienna-based independent builder,
real app screens, and an honest verdict. Do not copy another creator’s exact
phrasing, wardrobe, set, music, logo, character, or edit pattern.

## Series identity

- **Creator:** Fathy, presented as a curious, self-aware Vienna app builder.
- **Presenter:** the creator’s Higgsfield Soul/AI clone when synthetic footage is
  needed; disclose “AI-assisted creator” in the bio or caption.
- **Tone:** playful, observant, deadpan, warm, slightly embarrassed that the app
  exists.
- **Series promise:** “I build polished apps for problems that are too specific
  to deserve a product team.”
- **Soft close:** “If you want to try it, check the link in my bio.”

## Runtime and structure

Target 75–105 seconds for the first season. A 60-second cut and a 20-second
result cut can be made from the same edit. Seedance clips are short building
blocks; the finished episode is assembled from multiple clips, voiceover, real
screen recordings, and captions.

| Time | Story beat | What the viewer needs |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–6s | Cold open | See the real problem and understand why it is annoying. |
| 6–18s | Personal story | Hear what happened to the creator, not a generic product claim. |
| 18–32s | The proposed solution | Explain the app idea and the intentionally ridiculous product decision. |
| 32–48s | Build/process | Show sketches, rejected names, code/UI decisions, or a physical test. |
| 48–78s | App walkthrough | Use a clean screen recording; show the actual input, action, and result. |
| 78–94s | Honest verdict | Say what it does not do and whether it is genuinely useful. |
| 94–105s | Soft CTA | “If you want to try it, check the link in my bio.” End without hard selling. |

The story should feel complete even if the viewer never downloads anything.
The CTA is an invitation to investigate, not the reason for the video.

## Production rules

1. Record the creator’s voiceover and real app interaction first. Use AI video
   for the Soul presenter, atmospheric cutaways, or impossible visual jokes—not
   for readable app UI or fake feature demonstrations.
2. Capture the app on a clean simulator/device with fictional, privacy-safe
   inputs. Never ask Seedance to render exact interface text; generated text and
   buttons are unreliable.
3. Use one problem per episode. Explain only the decisions that help the viewer
   understand the product or the joke.
4. Include one failed idea, limitation, or awkward truth. This prevents the
   piece from becoming a disguised ad.
5. Show the result for long enough to read. Let the personality react to it.
6. The Soul is a recurring host, not a fake customer testimonial. Do not invent
   downloads, user reviews, App Store approval, or reactions from other people.
7. If a build uses manual input, say manual. If it uses on-device Vision,
   describe it as a photo check, not as knowing the truth. If the app is not
   publicly downloadable, say so.

## Dog Name Guesser pilot — Fluffers

### Working title

**I built an app to guess a dog’s name, and this one is Fluffers**

### Voiceover draft

> I have a problem whenever I meet a dog. I immediately try to guess its name
> before the owner tells me. Not because I have any evidence—mostly because a
> very fluffy dog looks like it should have a very specific name.
>
> This dog is called Fluffers, and that made me wonder whether I could turn the
> completely unscientific guessing process into an app.
>
> So I built Dog Name Guesser. You can give the committee a photo, adjust two
> extremely important scientific variables—fluff and seriousness—and type your
> accusation. For Fluffers, I wanted to see whether the app would accept the
> obvious answer or try to make the situation worse.
>
> The photo step is optional. The current build can perform an on-device Vision
> photo check and propose a silly name, but it cannot actually know a dog’s real
> name. That would require either the owner telling us or a level of canine
> bureaucracy Apple has not approved yet.
>
> I set Fluff to ten, seriousness to two, enter “Fluffers,” and present the name.
> The official result is: Fluffers is an excellent fluffy name. This is not a
> breakthrough in artificial intelligence. It is a formal way to be confidently
> wrong about a dog you have just met.
>
> Is it useful? Only if you need to turn a dog encounter into a tiny committee
> hearing. If you want to try it, check the link in my bio.

### Shot list

1. **Cold open:** Fathy notices Fluffers, silently studies the dog, then points
   at the dog as if identifying a suspect. Use a consenting owner and dog.
2. **Personal problem:** Soul presenter in a Vienna street or park, explaining
   the name-guessing habit directly to camera.
3. **Idea:** notebook or whiteboard with “photo + fluff + seriousness = name
   accusation”; include one crossed-out ridiculous name.
4. **Build:** close-ups of the phone, a slider being tuned, a simple UI sketch,
   and the app icon. Keep any code as texture, not as fake proof of complexity.
5. **Real demo:** open Dog Name Guesser, choose the Fluffers photo, show the
   on-device photo finding, set the two sliders, type “Fluffers,” and tap
   “Present the name.”
6. **Payoff:** hold on the result card, then cut to Fluffers looking away. The
   dog’s non-response is the reaction; do not subtitle invented thoughts.
7. **Honest verdict:** presenter admits that the app guesses from vibes and
   does not know the dog’s actual name.
8. **Close:** phone in hand, calm delivery: “If you want to try it, check the
   link in my bio.”

### On-screen captions

- `I HAVE A DOG-NAME PROBLEM`
- `THIS IS FLUFFERS`
- `SO I BUILT AN APP`
- `FLUFF: 10`
- `SERIOUSNESS: 2`
- `OFFICIAL RESULT: FLUFFERS`
- `IT DOES NOT KNOW THE DOG’S REAL NAME`
- `TRY IT → LINK IN BIO`

### Caption copy

I built an app for the moment before a dog’s owner tells you its name.
Fluffers survived the committee hearing. The app does not know a dog’s real
name—it just makes the guess feel official. If you want to try it, the link is
in my bio. What would you have guessed?

## App selection rule for the season

An app earns a long episode only if it has:

- a problem that can be acted out in the first six seconds;
- a build decision worth explaining;
- a visible interaction and result;
- a limitation that can be stated honestly; and
- a reason for viewers to submit their own example.

Dog Name Guesser passes this test. Thin apps that only produce one line from
two sliders should remain short-form jokes or be combined with a stronger
concept. Health-adjacent apps need a separate review before they receive a
creator-story episode.
